The pitch

Parental Genres & Guardrails

Two features that make Roblox safer for young players: Play Picks, where parents curate the genres their kids can even see, and a phonetic-aware profanity guardrail that catches the disguised swearing today's filters miss. I'm bringing them because I study cybersecurity and I'm on Roblox almost every night — in one lab project I perturbed text with misspellings and swaps to fool an AI classifier, the exact move a kid uses to slip hostile language past a chat filter. Defending against it is just the inverse of the attack I already studied.

The problem

Two gaps that hit young players hardest

Gap 1 · Discovery is too blunt

Age ratings aren't taste

A rating answers “is this game allowed for 8+?” It can't answer “does this family want their 8-year-old seeing shooters or horror?” Young kids browse a dashboard that surfaces everything in their age band, and one scary recommendation is a single tap away.

Gap 2 · Filters lose the arms race

Phonetics beat the word list

Profanity filters match exact words, so kids route around them — swapping characters (0 for o, 1 for i), adding spaces, or using homophones. The filter sees nonsense; the other kid reads the insult.

Parents can't shape what their kids actually see, and our word filters are beaten by the oldest trick online — spelling it differently.

Who this is for: players aged 5–14 first, their guardians, and the creators who lose trust when a young player has a bad experience.

My lens

Borrowing “defense in depth” from security

In security we never trust a single wall — we layer controls so that if one fails, the next still catches the harm. Safety for kids should work the same way.

1
Shape

Control what's allowed in

Let parents curate the genres their kids can even see — prevention by shaping the environment, not patching it afterward.

2
Normalize & detect

Match what it sounds like, not what it's spelled like

Don't match raw text — normalize out the obfuscation first, then detect. This is how you beat phonetic evasion.

3
Respond

Low-friction reporting

When something slips through, make it one tap to report, with context already attached.

Feature 1 · Shape

“Play Picks” — parent-curated genres

Parents toggle the genres each child (5–14) can play. Only allowed genres populate that child's Discover, Search, and charts — everything else is filtered out before it's ever shown. Age-band defaults get them started; parents adjust by values, not just age.

Why it's different from age ratings: ratings are a floor set by Roblox; Play Picks is a dial set by the family. It turns a one-size-fits-all age gate into a parent's actual preferences, and it works on discovery — so the off-limits games never tempt the kid in the first place.

Feature 2 · Normalize & detect

A phonetic-aware profanity guardrail

The fix for filter evasion isn't a longer blocklist — it's normalizing the text before you match it, so a disguised word like “h8” and its spaced-out version “h a t e” collapse to the same word the filter already knows.

How it works

Normalize, then match

  • Strip spacing, repeats and symbols; map leetspeak (0→o, 1→i, @→a, !→i).
  • Convert to a “sounds-like” phonetic form to catch homophones and creative spellings.
  • Match that against the blocklist + a model, then keep learning as kids invent new spellings.
Why me

The inverse of an attack I studied

My adversarial-testing project perturbed text — swaps, misspellings, spacing — to flip a classifier's output. That's the same evasion kids use on chat filters. I've already practiced the attack; this guardrail is the defense.

Treat the filter like any detection system: attackers (and kids) adapt, so it has to keep updating — a blocklist you set once is already out of date.
How I'd measure it

Success metrics & guardrails

A safety feature can “win” by making the platform worse to use — so I'd pair a north star with guardrails that protect the experience and the creators.

★ North star
Fewer young players exposed to age-inappropriate content or obfuscated profanity per active week
Feature signals
% of guardians who set genre preferences · catch-rate on a red-team set of disguised profanity · report-completion for under-13s
Guardrails
No tanking of young-player time in allowed genres · filter false-positive rate under threshold · small-creator impact watched
Shipping it

What I'd ship first — and what I'd cut

Ship firstDefer

MVP

  • Play Picks for ages 5–9 (highest parental demand), genre toggles with age-band defaults, applied to Discover + Search.
  • Phonetic filter on text chat, English first, tuned conservatively.
  • Instrument everything to prove impact before expanding.

Deliberately deferred

  • Per-game overrides (start at genre level — simpler, lower-maintenance).
  • Phonetic filtering in voice chat and other languages (separate, harder models).
  • Guardian dashboards & weekly digests (phase 2).
